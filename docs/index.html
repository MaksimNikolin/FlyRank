Abstract

Some pages already rank in positions 1–20 yet earn far fewer clicks than their position usually brings; this paper asks which of them keep doing so, and which an editor should review first. Using an anonymized 30,000-page slice of FlyRank's production search data (9,664 eligible pages from 28 clients), plus a one-month replication check on the ~79M-row internship warehouse, I labelled a page as under-capturing when its click-through rate (CTR) in the next 30 days fell below half the pooled CTR of its position bucket. A random forest that sees only the preceding month was compared with a transparent rule on clients it never saw, under client-grouped and time-forward validation, with explicit leakage tests. On unseen clients the model measured precision@50 of 0.80 against 0.70 for the rule (label base rate 37.8%), a gap that narrows to 0.74 vs 0.70 in the stricter time-forward test; separately, pages with a gap two months running stayed under 66% of the time, against 50% after a single month. The result is a decision-support review queue with reason codes and human-review rules, not evidence that rewriting a title recovers clicks.

1Introduction: a cheap opportunity hidden in noise

Picture an editor on a Monday with time for twenty pages. One page sits at position 4 on Google and is shown a hundred thousand times a month, yet earns a fraction of the clicks its neighbours at position 4 get. The visibility is already paid for; the snippet or the match to search intent is the likely problem. That page looks like the cheapest win in the portfolio.

The obvious rule is "flag every page whose CTR is below half of what its position usually gets." It works, but it hides a cost: CTR over a month is noisy, and in this data about 4 in 10 flagged pages were no longer under-capturing the month after (half, when the gap had appeared only that month). Editors working from the raw rule spend hours on pages that would have recovered anyway.

So the research question is narrow and practical: can a model that weighs several signals rank the lasting gaps higher than the rule does, on clients it has never seen? And if it can, how should that ranking turn into editorial actions without overclaiming?

FlyRank's own public report on AI-driven SEO already observed a steep drop in CTR from top positions to deep ones. This work starts one step later: within a given position, which pages fall short, and does it last?

What I excluded, and why

  • Positions outside 1–20: too few clicks to compare CTR fairly (20,256 of 30,000 pages remain).
  • Pages under 1,000 impressions in 90 days, or under 100 in either modeled month: a CTR built on a handful of views is noise (9,664 remain).
  • Every column that contains or is computed from the label month: last-30-day metrics as features, 90-day totals, the dataset's trend fields, and the "days since last update" snapshot.
  • Client and page IDs as features: they are used only to keep whole clients out of training.

3Methodology

Days 61–90earliest month
Days 31–60features
Last 30 dayslabel
time-forward training featuresmain features · time-forward training labelnever a feature

Label (observed in a later window). A page stays under when its CTR in the label month is below half of the pooled CTR of its narrow position bucket (1–3, 3–5, 5–10, 10–15, 15–20) in that same month. I use narrow buckets because CTR was not monotonic across coarse tiers in this data: the "positions 11–20" tier pooled at roughly the same CTR as "4–10".

Features come only from the month before the label, plus static context: impressions, clicks and sessions (logged), CTR, CTR relative to its bucket, average position, content age, word count, intent and content type.

Baseline, frozen before any model was trained: flag when CTR is below half of the bucket CTR, score by missed clicks (the clicks the page would have earned at bucket CTR), reason code LOW_CTR_FOR_POSITION.

Models. Logistic regression as the readable reference; a random forest (300 trees, minimum leaf 20) as the main model; shallow gradient boosting as a check; and the playbook ranking, forest probability × missed clicks, which ranks by expected missed clicks rather than by certainty alone.

Validation. Five folds grouped by client, so every test client is unseen. Then a stricter time-forward + grouped design: train on the earliest month → features month, test on the features month → label month, again on unseen clients. Every metric sits next to its base rate. Precision@50 is the main metric because an editor works the top of the list; the share of all missed clicks captured by the top 50 guards against a list full of tiny pages.

Assumptions. Pooled bucket CTR is a fair "expected CTR" within this portfolio; 100 impressions are enough to estimate a month's CTR; the 90-day average position is close enough to the month's position. The last one is tested below.

Leakage checks

A model that quietly reads the answer looks brilliant and is worthless, so I attacked my own pipeline. If adding a leaky column did not send the score toward perfect, the test harness itself would be broken.

Gaps that repeat are the ones that last

What the errors look like

About 23% of the forest's top-10% picks recovered on their own. Those false positives were thinner pages (median about 950 impressions vs 1,600 for correct picks), and both groups had a median of zero clicks, so for small pages "zero clicks" is partly chance. The worst individual errors shared an impression swing: confident false positives lost 70–90% of their impressions while clicks held, and the biggest misses roughly tripled their impressions while CTR halved. As a group the swing is weak, so I treat "query-mix shift" as a hypothesis, not a finding.

On the warehouse month, with different windows and five simpler features, a logistic regression on unseen clients measured ROC-AUC 0.76 and precision@50 of 0.76 against a 50% base rate. That's a separate setup, not a benchmark of the model above, but it points the same way.

5Limitations and honest framing

What this work does not show: that rewriting a title or refreshing a page recovers clicks. No edit was made or randomized. Pages with a recorded update in the last 30 days kept their gaps about as often as others (60% vs 61%), and because those updates were not aimed at CTR, that is not evidence either way.
  • A small, uneven client base. 28 clients, and one of them holds 39% of modeled pages. Precision@50 for the model ranged from 0.62 to 0.94 across folds; differences of a few points are within that noise.
  • A slice, not the full warehouse. The main results come from a 30,000-page slice. The warehouse check covers one month with half-month windows; it supports the direction, not the exact numbers.
  • One known window overlap. Average position is a 90-day figure that overlaps the label month. Removing it barely changes the score, but bucket assignment still depends on it.
  • No query-level signal. The model can't see which queries a page is shown for, which is the likely cause of its worst errors.
  • Portfolio-relative expectations. "Expected CTR" is pooled within these clients, not an industry benchmark, and search-result features such as ads or answer boxes aren't observed.
  • Time. Three months of one export. Seasonality and search-engine changes are untested.

6Ranked recommendations: the action playbook

The queue scores the latest month for the month ahead (9,752 pages) and ranks by forest probability × missed clicks. On unseen clients, one month ahead, that ranking measured precision@50 of 0.71 vs 0.70 for the rule and captured 0.42 vs 0.39 of missed clicks: about the same order. Its added value is the reason codes and actions that tell an editor what to do. In order:

  1. Rewrite title and meta first on persistent gaps at positions 1–5REWRITE_TITLE_META · GAP_TWO_MONTHS + TOP_POSITION

    Visible and losing the click two months running; the snippet is the most likely lever. 24 of the top 100.

  2. At positions 5–20, check intent before polishing the snippetREWRITE_TITLE_META + INTENT_CHECK

    The page may answer a different question than the searcher's. The largest group: 38 of the top 100.

  3. Give aging pages a freshness review on topREWRITE_TITLE_META + FRESHNESS_REVIEW · AGING_PAGE

    Pages older than a year carried more gaps and kept them longer. 14 of the top 100.

  4. Look at the query mix before editing pages whose impressions halved or doubledCHECK_QUERY_MIX · CAUTION_IMPRESSION_SHIFT

    These produced the model's worst errors. 13 of the top 100.

  5. Re-check one-month gaps next month instead of editingRECHECK_NEXT_MONTH

    About half of them don't repeat. 8 of the top 100 (3 more are watch-only, with no current gap).

Cost and value. Under my assumed editing times, the top 50 is about 23 editor hours and holds roughly 7,800 missed clicks a month, against about 400 for 50 random gap pages. That concentration is partly by construction, since the ranking includes missed clicks. How many of those clicks an edit would win back has not been measured; that needs a held-back comparison group.

Human review and the no-go list

Every row is reviewed by a person, who checks the page's real queries, the live search results page, any caution codes, recent edits or redirects, and the client's brand and legal wording, and then logs the change. Never automate:

  • publishing title or meta rewrites, AI-written or otherwise;
  • deleting, noindexing, merging or redirecting pages;
  • rewriting body content of aging pages;
  • client-facing "scores" or judging editors by them;
  • promising click gains to a client;
  • acting on navigational, thin-data or impression-shift pages without the checks.

When to stop trusting the queue

Retrain or fall back to the rule when last month's realized precision@50 drops below 0.60, the share of under-capturing pages moves more than 10 points, any bucket's expected CTR shifts more than 25%, or feature drift (PSI) exceeds 0.2; retrain every quarter regardless. To finally measure whether edits help, hold back a random ~20% of edit candidates each month and compare.

7Reproducibility

Everything runs from the public repository github.com/MaksimNikolin/FlyRank, with fixed seed 42, scikit-learn 1.9 and pandas. Clone, install the requirements, and run the notebooks top to bottom; the starter slice ships with the repo. The warehouse check needs your own Hugging Face read token and runs in Colab.
